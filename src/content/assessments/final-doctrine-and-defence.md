## The brief

> State, in one page, what makes an email worth sending. Then defend one you
> would actually send.

Twelve weeks each named a different way an email can fail. This
assessment asks you to pull that into one position of your own, not a
checklist. The doctrine is your account of what makes an email worth a
reader's reply, argued in your own words rather than repeated week by
week. The defence is where that doctrine gets tested against a real
email, one you would genuinely send rather than a hypothetical picked
because it obviously passes, and checked for where it falls short of your
own standard.

## What you submit

A one-page doctrine, and a defence of one real email (yours, redacted if
needed) run against it, including, honestly, where the email doesn't
fully hold up. A doctrine with no tension against the defended email
reads as unexamined, not as a clean pass.
