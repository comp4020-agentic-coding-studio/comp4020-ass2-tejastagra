## The brief

> State, in one page, what makes an email worth sending. Then defend one you
> would actually send.

Twelve weeks of individual failure modes need synthesising into a position,
not a checklist. The doctrine is your own account of what makes an email
worth a reader's reply, argued from the semester rather than restating it
week by week. The defence is where that doctrine gets tested against a real
email — one you would genuinely send, not a hypothetical picked because it
obviously passes — examined for where it falls short of your own standard.

## What you submit

A one-page doctrine, and a defence of one real email (yours, redacted if
needed) run against it — including, honestly, where the email doesn't fully
hold up. A doctrine with no tension against the defended email reads as
unexamined, not as a clean pass.
