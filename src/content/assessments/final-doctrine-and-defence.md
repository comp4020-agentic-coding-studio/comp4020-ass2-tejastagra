## What the assignment is

Pick two real people you would genuinely want to email. Write a cold
email to each one.

The two emails must be for two clearly different reasons. One of the two
reasons must involve a real ask. For example, asking for a job, an
introduction or a favour. The other reason does not need an ask at all.
For example, genuinely appreciating someone whose work you admire, with
nothing you want from them.

Write both emails as if you would actually send them. You do not
actually send them.

## What this tests

Whether you can adapt what the course teaches to two very different
situations, rather than applying one formula to everything. A job
request and a message with no ask are close to opposite emails. They
need different structure, different length and a different sense of
what the reader owes you, if anything. Writing both well, for two real
people, is the point of the assignment.

## What you submit

For each of your two people, submit all of the following.

1. **Who they are and why you are writing to them.** A few sentences on
   who this person is and why you picked them.
2. **What you actually want from them, if anything.** State the ask
   plainly, or state that there is no ask.
3. **One line on why your two reasons are genuinely different.** Say
   what makes this pair a real contrast, not two versions of the same
   email.
4. **The email you would send, written in full.** Use the email-block
   format below, not a description of what the email would say.
5. **A short justification of your main choices.** Explain what you were
   doing in the email, how it fits how this specific person would read
   it, and which course ideas you were drawing on.

Submitted through Canvas under the Assignments page.

## What a good submission looks like

Here is one entry, for the ask-based email.

Who and why: a former lecturer of mine now works at a company I want to
join. I am writing to ask if she can refer me for an open role there.

What you want: a referral for a specific open role.

Why this reason is different from the other email: this email asks for
something concrete and time-bound. The other email asks for nothing and
has no deadline attached to it.

Email:

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Referral for the Data Analyst role at [company]</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>Hi [name], I saw [company] has an open Data Analyst role and I'd like to apply. Would you be willing to refer me? I've attached my CV. Happy to send anything else you need, and no worries if this isn't something you can do.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Justification: I named the exact role and attached the CV so she has
everything she needs to act in one email, rather than a back and forth.
I gave her an easy way to say no, since a referral puts her name on the
line and she should not feel cornered into it. This follows the course's
point that a specific, bounded ask is easier to say yes to than an open
one.
