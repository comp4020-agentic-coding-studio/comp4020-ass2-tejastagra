## What this assignment tests

This assignment tests two separate skills. The first is diagnosis: can
you say, in your own words, exactly what is wrong with a cold email.
Point at the specific line or structural choice causing the problem.
Don't just say "it's too pushy" or "it doesn't feel personal".

The second is repair. Can you turn that diagnosis into a redrafted email
that actually resolves it. A correct diagnosis with no working redraft
is only half the assignment. A redraft that reads better but doesn't fix
what you diagnosed is not a pass either.

There is no fixed formula here. Each of the ten emails fails for its own
reasons. The redraft has to answer those specific reasons, not apply a
generic template.

## What you submit

Ten entries, one per email. Each entry must contain, in this order:

1. **The original email**, redacted, reproduced in full.
2. **A diagnosis** of what's wrong with it. For every problem you
   identify, point at the specific line or structural choice responsible,
   not a general impression of the email's tone or intent.
3. **A fix for each problem**, plus a brief justification of why that fix
   works, tied to something the course has taught (not just an assertion
   that it's "better").
4. **A full redraft** of the email that applies every fix from point 3 at
   once. The redraft must be a complete, sendable email, not a bullet list
   of changes or a partial rewrite of one sentence.

Submitted through Canvas under the Assignments page. There is no fixed
word count for the diagnosis or justification: write enough to make the
reasoning clear and stop. As a rough guide, most entries will run to a
few sentences of diagnosis and justification per problem identified, plus
the redrafted email itself.

## How to approach it

Read each email as its recipient would, in the first few seconds, before
you write anything down. Point the diagnosis at the email's own words,
not at cold email in general. Quote or closely paraphrase the specific
phrase or structural choice you're objecting to.

Before you submit an entry, check the redraft against your own
diagnosis, line by line. If you named three problems, the redraft needs
to fix all three. Two out of three plus a general tidy-up is not enough.

## What a good submission looks like

One entry done well looks like this.

Original email:

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Quick question</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>Hi, hope you're well. I'd love to pick your brain sometime about your career path, would you be free for a quick call?</p>
  </div>
</div>

Diagnosis: the second sentence asks for an open-ended call. It doesn't
say what the call is about or how long it would take. The reader has to
define the ask themselves before they can even agree to it. There is
also no stated reason for contacting this particular person. The email
reads as one of many identical requests rather than something written
for them.

Fix and justification: name the specific question and cap the time. This
moves the cost of defining the meeting from the reader to the sender,
which is the sender's job. Naming a concrete reason for contacting this
person gives the reader something to weigh the request against.

Redraft:

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [redacted]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> 15 minutes on your move from engineering to product</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>Hi [name], I've been following your move from engineering into product at [company] and I'm considering a similar shift. Would you have 15 minutes in the next couple of weeks to talk through how you made that transition? Happy to work around your schedule.</p>
  </div>
</div>

That redraft resolves both problems named in the diagnosis: the ask is
now specific and time-boxed, and the reason for contacting this person is
stated rather than implied.
