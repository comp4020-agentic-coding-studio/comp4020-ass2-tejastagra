Most cold email gets deleted unread. This lecture looks at three cases where
it didn't: a stranger read a cold email, believed it, and acted on it. The
rest of the course is built on the question those three cases answer.

<Callout title="Before the lecture">
  This is just to get a quick understanding before the lecture. The
  examples and the rest are in the lecture, so it's advisable you attend.
</Callout>

## In brief

The failure mode this week sets up isn't in this week. It's the baseline
every later week's failure violates: can a reader who owes you nothing
decide what to do with your email in a few seconds? Ghulati, Janoch and
Field all cleared that bar, and none of them did it with charm. Every
failure named from week 2 onward is a specific way of losing those few
seconds instead.

## Outline

- why cold email still gets used at all, despite most of it going unread
- Ghulati → Cuban: a plain problem statement and a sizeable ask, no charm
- Janoch → investors: why the second, longer email outperformed the first
- Field's early Figma outreach: cold email as user acquisition, not just
  fundraising
- what the three have in common — the reader is given enough to decide with
- how the next eleven weeks are organised: individual failures, then the
  reader's economics, then genre confusion, then the ethical edge
