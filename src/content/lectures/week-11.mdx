Week 4 argued for specificity that would be false for a different reader.

## In brief

This week's failure is automation flattening: a sequence can send a
thousand emails in the time a one-to-one version takes to write. It
can't hold the kind of specificity week 4 asked for, because that
specificity has to be true of one reader and false of the rest. It
matters because a template merging in a name and a company isn't the same
as knowing anything about the person receiving it, and a writer who treats
volume and reply rate as the same thing will optimise the wrong one.
