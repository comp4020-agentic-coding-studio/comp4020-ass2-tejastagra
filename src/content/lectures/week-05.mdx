The last two weeks looked at what an email asks for and how it pretends to
know the reader. This week is about something a reader judges before
either of those registers at all: the shape of the email on the screen.

  This is just to get a quick understanding before the lecture. The
  examples and the rest are in the lecture, so it's advisable you attend.
</Callout>

## In brief

This week's failure is unstructured length: an email whose shape, not its
content, tells the reader how much time it will cost before they've read a
word of it. A long email built around one visible ask can read faster than
a short, rambling one, because shape carries information on its own.
Readers give up on the shapeless version before judging whether the idea
inside it was any good, which is why the fix is restructuring around the
ask rather than just cutting words.
