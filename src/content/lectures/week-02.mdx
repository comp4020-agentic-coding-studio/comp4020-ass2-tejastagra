Week 1 established that a cold email has to give the reader enough to
decide with. This lecture argues that decision usually gets made before
the body is ever opened, at the subject line, and that most writers treat
the subject line as a label on the pitch rather than as the pitch itself.

<Callout title="Before the lecture">
  This is just to get a quick understanding before the lecture. The
  examples and the rest are in the lecture, so it's advisable you attend.
</Callout>

## In brief

This week's failure is the subject line that describes the email instead
of stating the fact inside it. "Following up" makes the reader open the
message to find out what it's about; "Grant deadline moved to Friday"
lets them decide without opening anything. That gap matters because most
subject lines fail this test while their writers believe they're being
appropriately restrained rather than costing the reader a wasted decision.

## Outline

- the open-or-skip decision happens before the body is read
- descriptive vs. self-sufficient subject lines, and what "self-sufficient"

- why unresolved ambiguity forces the open a subject line should prevent
- worked example: a "Following up" that answers nothing, rewritten to
  answer all three
- what published data on "Quick question"-style subject lines shows about
  how common this is
