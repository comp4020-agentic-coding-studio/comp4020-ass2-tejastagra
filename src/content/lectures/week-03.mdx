The subject line gets an email opened. What happens after that usually
comes down to the ask. This week's failure mode is the vague ask, and the
argument is that it doesn't fail because it's rude. It fails because it
hands work to the reader that the reader never agreed to do.

<Callout title="Before the lecture">
  This is just to get a quick understanding before the lecture. The
  examples and the rest are in the lecture, so it's advisable you attend.
</Callout>

## In brief

A vague ask forces the reader to work out what you actually want, how long
it will take, and whether it's worth their calendar, before they've even
decided whether to help. That cost lands the same way whether the email is
polite or blunt, which is why politeness and cost turn out to be different
things. Readers usually give up on a vague ask before they've judged
whether the idea behind it is any good. The fix is to price the ask, not to
write around it more nicely.
