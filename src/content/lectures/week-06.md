Weeks 2 through 5 named individual craft failures. This lecture steps back
and gives the reader's decision an actual shape: every opened email gets
weighed against what replying costs, and most cold email is optimised for
the sender's side of that weighing and never touches the reader's. The
full workthrough is on the slide deck linked above.

## Summary

Opening an email costs almost nothing. Replying does — five minutes,
sometimes fifteen, sometimes a commitment that outlives the email entirely.
Every reader runs some version of: is what I get from replying worth what
replying costs me? The framework this lecture uses to make that legible is

    reply likelihood ≈ relevance × specificity ÷ length

not as arithmetic, but as a way to name which term is weakest before
rewriting anything. Relevance asks whether this concerns something the
reader already cares about. Specificity asks whether they can act on it
without needing more information. Length asks how much of their time it
costs to find that out.

Composite example, illustrating the pattern:

> Loved what you're building — really impressive traction. Any advice you
> can share on hiring would be hugely appreciated. Would you have 30
> minutes sometime?

Four months, no reply. Length is fine — the email is short. Specificity is
weak: "any advice" has no bounded action. Relevance is what actually
fails: nothing explains why this particular reader, out of the fifty other
emails making the same kind of request, should spend 30 minutes on this
one. Fixing the wrong term here — trimming it further, or adding more
compliments — would waste the rewrite; the framework's value is in pointing
at relevance as the term to actually work on.
