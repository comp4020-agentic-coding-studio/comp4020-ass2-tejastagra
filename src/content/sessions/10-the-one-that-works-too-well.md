Everything this course has taught so far makes an email more likely to be
read and answered. None of that is free of an edge: specificity can become
fabricated familiarity, a real deadline can become an invented one, and
personalisation built at scale can simulate a one-to-one relationship that
doesn't exist. The techniques don't change; what changes is whether the
reader is being told the truth about what they're looking at.

This week draws the line explicitly rather than leaving students to find it
by accident. A deadline you didn't set is not a deadline. A "researched your
company" line generated from a script run against a thousand companies is not
research, even if it's accurate.

> I'm closing out this round by Friday, so wanted to see if you'd be
> interested in getting in before then.

The round in question had no fixed close date; the deadline existed only
in this email, invented to move the reader faster than the facts justified.
A real deadline genuinely changes reply behaviour — that's exactly why
inventing one crosses a line the rest of this course's craft doesn't.

## Exercise

Take a technique from an earlier week — specificity, a stated deadline,
personalisation — and write two versions of an email using it: one honest,
one that crosses into manipulation. Identify the exact sentence where the
second version stops being true.
