Every reader runs the same rough calculation on an opened email: what does
replying cost me, against what happens if I don't. Most cold emails lose
that calculation because the writer only ever addressed their own side of
it — what the sender wants, with no sense of what a reply is worth to the
person granting it.

This week's lecture works through that calculation as a framework — relevance
to the reader, specificity of the ask, divided by the length it takes to
convey both — and carries the semester's one slide deck, built around a real
worked example.

> Loved what you're building — really impressive traction. Any advice you
> can share on hiring would be hugely appreciated. Would you have 30
> minutes sometime?

Four months, no reply. Specificity is weak (no bounded action) and length
is fine (short), but relevance is what actually fails here: nothing
explains why this reader, specifically, should spend 30 minutes on a
request that fifty other emails in their inbox are also making.

## Exercise

Take an email that got no reply. Run it through the framework from the
lecture and identify which term was weakest: relevance, specificity, or
length. Rewrite only that term, and nothing else, then compare the two
versions side by side.
