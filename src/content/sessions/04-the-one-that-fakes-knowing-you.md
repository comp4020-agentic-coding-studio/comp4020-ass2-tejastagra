"I loved your recent post on X" mentions the reader, but it isn't
personalisation in the sense that matters, because it could be sent to
anyone. Real specificity commits to a detail that would be wrong for a
different reader: a number from their actual work, a decision they
actually made, a consequence that follows from something only they did.

The tell is almost always the second sentence. The first sentence can be
generated by a template with your name dropped in; the second sentence is
where a template has nothing left to say and either goes vague or stops.

> I really enjoyed your recent talk on scaling engineering teams — it's
> clear you know this space inside and out. I wanted to reach out because I
> think our product could be a great fit for what you're building.

The first sentence could describe any talk by any engineering leader. The
second drops the reader entirely and pivots to a generic pitch. Real
specificity would have named what was actually said in the talk and
connected it, in one line, to something true only of this reader.

## Exercise

Find a line of "personalisation" in a cold email addressed to you and try to
rewrite it so it would also be true, word for word, of ten other recipients.
If you can, it wasn't personalisation. Then write a version that would be
false for anyone else.
