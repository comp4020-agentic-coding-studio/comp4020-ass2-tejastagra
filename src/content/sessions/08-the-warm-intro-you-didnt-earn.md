Asking a stranger for their time and asking a connection to spend their own
credibility introducing you to someone else are different requests with
different costs. A cold ask risks the reader's ten minutes. A warm-intro
request risks the connector's relationship with the person they're
introducing you to — a cost most writers never mention, let alone address.

Writing both the same way — "would you be open to connecting me with X" sent
with the same effort as a cold email — treats the connector's risk as if it
weren't there. The fix is naming it: what you'll say to the third party, why
this specific introduction, and an easy way for the connector to say no
without it being awkward.

> Would you be open to connecting me with [Name]? I think what I'm working
> on could be really relevant to them.

This is addressed to the connector but reads identically to a cold email
addressed to the stranger — no mention of what the connector would say on
the sender's behalf, no acknowledgment that the introduction spends their
own relationship, and no easy way to decline.

## Exercise

Find an intro request you've sent or received that reads like a cold email
with a name swapped in. Rewrite it to name the connector's actual risk and
give them a clean way to decline.
