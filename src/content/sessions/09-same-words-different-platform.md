The failures this course names so far assume email's particular constraints:
a subject line, no read receipt, an inbox the reader chose to check. A
LinkedIn message is triaged inside a feed the reader didn't choose to open
right now. A DM arrives inside a channel with its own social expectations
about response time. Copying a cold email into any of these and expecting it
to read the same way ignores the environment it now sits in.

This week is not about writing three versions of everything. It's about
recognising which of this course's failure modes still apply on a different
platform, and which change shape entirely — length tolerance is not the same
on LinkedIn as in email, and a follow-up cadence that's normal in a DM thread
would be aggressive as email.

> Hi [Name], I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out
> regarding a potential opportunity to collaborate. Please find my
> availability below for a call this week.

Sent as a LinkedIn DM, this reads as noticeably more formal than the
channel expects, and its length assumes an inbox the reader chose to check
rather than a feed they're scrolling past. Nothing here was adapted for
where it landed.

## Exercise

Take one message you've written and adapt it, deliberately, for the two
platforms you didn't write it for. Name what had to change beyond length —
tone, cadence, or what counts as an acceptable ask.
