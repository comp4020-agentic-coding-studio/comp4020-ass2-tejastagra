# Week 7 — Follow-Ups

The reader's math from last week doesn't reset when a first email goes
unanswered. It gets asked again.

---

## What this lecture covers

- why "bumping" isn't a follow-up
- what a follow-up actually needs to add
- a composite bump email and why it fails
- the honest alternative when nothing has changed

---

## What "bumping" actually is

"Just bumping this to the top of your inbox" is not a follow-up.

It's a second copy of the original cost of being read, with none of the
original content's chance of being useful.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Persistence isn't free for the reader, even when it's free for the writer
to send.

---

## What a follow-up needs

A follow-up that works adds something the first email didn't have:

- a new fact
- a narrower ask
- a deadline that's now real

Not just a request to reconsider a decision the reader already made once.

cost of attention again, on no new grounds.

---

## The honest alternative

Replace "just bumping this" with the one fact that's actually changed
since the first email.

If nothing has changed: the honest conclusion is that it isn't time to
follow up yet.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Talk to your neighbour

Find a follow-up you've sent or received. Name the one new fact in it. If
there isn't one, discuss what would have needed to change first.

---

## Recap

- a follow-up repeats the first email's cost unless it adds something new
- new information vs. repeated urgency is the whole distinction
- no new fact means no follow-up yet, not a gentler bump
