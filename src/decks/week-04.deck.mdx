# Week 4 — Personalisation and Specificity

A compliment that could go to anyone doesn't read as attention. It reads as
the opposite.

---

## What this lecture covers

- performed personalisation vs. real personalisation
- the second-sentence tell
- the ten-recipients test
- a composite example, worked through both ways

---

## Performed vs. real

Performed personalisation names something true about the reader that
requires no specific knowledge to say — a job title, a company name, a
recent headline anyone could find in ten seconds.

Real personalisation uses a detail that shows the writer actually looked,
and connects that detail to the ask.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Flattery that could go to anyone reads as effort spent proving it went to
someone.

---

## The second-sentence tell

The compliment sentence is decoration. The second sentence is where the
email either connects that compliment to something specific, or reveals
that it was never going to.

If sentence two could follow any compliment about any person in the same
role, the personalisation in sentence one wasn't real.

---

## The ten-recipients test

Could this exact email, with only the name swapped, go to ten different
people in the same role and still make sense?

If yes, nothing about it was actually built for this reader — no matter how
warm the opening line sounds.

---

## The rewrite

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> The on-call rotation problem from your talk</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>In your talk you mentioned the on-call rotation breaking down past 30 engineers — we built the tool that fixes exactly that split, and I'd like to show you how.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Passes the ten-recipients test: this version can't go to anyone else.

## Talk to your neighbour

Find a cold email you've received that opens with a compliment. Does the
second sentence connect to it, or move on to something generic? Compare
findings.

---

## Recap

- performed personalisation names something true but generic about the reader
- the second-sentence tell exposes it: does the email return to the specific
  detail, or move past it
- the ten-recipients test is the same check from the other direction
