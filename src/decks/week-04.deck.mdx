# The One That Fakes Knowing You

Mentioning the reader isn't the same as knowing them.

---

## Two kinds of personalisation

"I loved your recent post on X" mentions the reader — but it could be sent
to anyone who wrote a post recently.

Real specificity commits to a detail that would be wrong for a different
reader: a number from their actual work, a decision they made, a
consequence that follows from something only they did.

---

## The second-sentence tell

A template can generate the first sentence with a name dropped in.

The second sentence is where the template has nothing left to say — it
either goes vague, or pivots straight to the pitch.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Loved your talk on scaling eng teams</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>I really enjoyed your recent talk on scaling engineering teams — it's clear you know this space inside and out. I wanted to reach out because I think our product could be a great fit for what you're building.</p>
  </div>
</div>

Sentence one: true of any talk by any engineering leader. Sentence two:
drops the reader entirely, pivots to a generic pitch.

---

## The test

Would this sentence, word for word, also be true of ten other recipients?

If yes, it's not personalisation — whatever it mentions by name.

If a second sentence can't survive that test without going vague, the
personalisation was performed, not real.

---

## What would have to change

Not more compliments. The second sentence would need to name what was
actually said in the talk, and connect it to something true only of this
reader — a detail that breaks if swapped into someone else's inbox.

---

## Recap

- personalisation that survives being sent to anyone isn't personalisation
- the tell is the second sentence, not the first
- the test: would this line still be true of ten other recipients?

---

## Exercise

Take a "personalised" line you've written or received. Apply the
ten-recipients test. Rewrite the second sentence so it fails the test on
purpose.
