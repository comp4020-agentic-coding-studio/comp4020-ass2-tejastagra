# Week 10 — Where Craft Becomes Manipulation

Everything taught this semester makes an email more likely to be read and
answered. None of it is free of an edge.

---

## What this lecture covers

- the edge every technique from this course carries
- what actually changes between craft and manipulation
- a composite email with a fabricated deadline
- a test to run against any technique before using it

---

## The edge

Specificity can become fabricated familiarity.

A real deadline can become an invented one.

Personalisation built at scale can simulate a one-to-one relationship that
doesn't exist.

---

## What actually changes

The techniques don't change. What changes is whether the reader is being
told the truth about what they're looking at.

A deadline you didn't set is not a deadline, even if it produces the same
urgency. A "researched your company" line generated from a script run
against a thousand companies is not research, even if it's accurate.

## Predict, then watch

Before the next slide: predict what's fabricated in an email that says
"closing the round Friday." Then check what the round's actual deadline
was.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Closing the round Friday</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>I'm closing out this round by Friday, so wanted to see if you'd be interested in getting in before then.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The round had no fixed close date. The deadline existed only in this
email, invented to move the reader faster than the facts justified.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

A real deadline works precisely because it's real — which is exactly why
inventing one crosses a line.

---

## Why this crosses a line the rest of the course doesn't

A real deadline genuinely changes reply behaviour — that's exactly why
inventing one works, and exactly why it crosses a line.

---

## The test

Would the reader still believe this if they knew exactly how it was
produced?

Apply it to any technique from the semester before using it.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Closing the round in three weeks</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>We're aiming to close this round by the end of the month based on the term sheets already signed. If that timeline works for you, I'd rather talk this week than next.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The honest version still creates urgency — it just ties that urgency to
something actually true and sourced from a real decision, rather than
inventing one for the email.

## Recap

- craft and manipulation use the same techniques; truthfulness is the
  difference
- a fabricated deadline borrows real urgency under false pretences
- the test: would the reader still believe it, knowing how it was made
