# Week 6 — The Reader's Reply Calculation

Why most cold emails lose a calculation the reader never announces they're
running.

---

## What this lecture covers

- the reader's actual cost-benefit math
- a framework for naming which term is weakest
- a redacted email that got four months of silence
- what would have had to change, and why

---

## The reader's actual math

Opening an email costs almost nothing. Replying to it does — five minutes,
sometimes fifteen, sometimes a commitment that outlives the email entirely.

Every reader runs some version of: **is what I get from replying worth what
replying costs me?**

Most cold emails are optimised for the sender's side of that question and
never touch the reader's.

---

## A framework, not a formula

```
reply likelihood ≈ relevance × specificity ÷ length
```

- relevance: does this concern something the reader already cares about
- specificity: can they act on it without more information
- length: how much of their time it costs to find that out

Not arithmetic. A way to name which term is weakest.

## Walking a real one

A redacted email that got silence, four months, no reply:

- opened with a compliment true of a hundred other companies
- asked for "any advice you can share" — no bounded action
- closed with a 30-minute ask and no stated reason it mattered to the reader

Relevance and specificity both failed. Length wasn't the problem.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A composite version of the same failure, for a case you can quote directly:

<div class="email-block">
  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient]</p>
    <p><strong>Subject:</strong> Quick question about hiring</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>Loved what you're building — really impressive traction. Any advice you can share on hiring would be hugely appreciated. Would you have 30 minutes sometime?</p>
  </div>
</div>

Same three failures as the real case: no bounded action, no reason this
particular reader was worth 30 minutes, and a compliment doing the work
relevance should be doing instead.

---

## What would have had to change

Not a better compliment. A named, checkable reason this reader specifically
was worth emailing, and an ask small enough to say yes to without thinking.

The framework doesn't ask for more effort. It asks for effort spent on the
term that's actually broken.

## Talk to your neighbour

Take an email you've received that got no reply from you. Which term failed

## Recap

- the reader's cost-benefit is real and usually invisible to the writer
- relevance, specificity, length — diagnose the weakest one before rewriting
  anything else
- fixing the wrong term wastes the rewrite
