# Week 2 — Subject Lines and the Open Decision

The decision usually gets made before the body is ever opened.

---

## What this lecture covers

- the mechanism: descriptive vs. self-sufficient subject lines
- the test for whether a subject line survives on its own
- a composite example, and the fix
- what published data says about how common this failure is

---

## The mechanism

A subject line that describes the email asks the reader to open it to find
out what it's for.

A subject line that states the specific, checkable thing inside lets the
reader decide correctly without opening anything.

---

## The test

Does the subject line survive being read on its own, stripped of the email
underneath it?

- "Introduction" — fails. Names a category, not this email.
- "Following up" — fails, for the same reason.
- "Grant deadline moved to Friday" — passes. Actionable without opening.

---

## What "survives on its own" requires

A reader can decide two things from the subject line alone:

1. does this concern them at all
2. does it need acting on now, or can it wait

A descriptive subject line answers neither, so the reader is left guessing
— and resolving a guess means opening the message anyway.

does it survive? Talk to your neighbour, then check your answer against
what the email underneath it actually says.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Ambiguity is a below-average strategy, even though it's the single most
reached-for choice in the language.

---

## The fix

Rewrite "Following up" as: *"Following up: still looking for 10 minutes on
the Q2 pricing proposal before Friday."*

Answers what it's about, why it's relevant now, and what happens if the
reader doesn't reply. The original answers none of the three.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Look this up

Find a "Quick question" or "Checking in" subject line in your own inbox —
sent or received. Strip the body away. Would a stranger know, from the
subject alone, whether it concerns them and whether it's urgent?

---

## Recap

- the open-or-skip decision happens at the subject line, before the body
- "self-sufficient" means relevance and urgency are decidable unopened
- category subject lines ("Quick question") underperform the average
  subject line in large-scale open-rate analyses, not just the best ones
