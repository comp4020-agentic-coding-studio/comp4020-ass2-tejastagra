# Week 9: Email, LinkedIn and DMs

Every failure named so far assumed email's particular constraints. What
happens when the same message moves somewhere else?

---

## What this lecture covers

- why platform changes the rules, not just the delivery method
- what carries across platforms, and what doesn't
- a composite DM that's formal in the wrong channel
- adapting a message beyond just trimming it

---

## Different environments, different rules

- a LinkedIn message gets skimmed inside a feed the reader didn't choose to
  open right now
- a DM arrives inside a channel with its own social expectations about
  response time
- an email arrives in an inbox the reader chose to check

Copying one into another and expecting it to read the same way ignores the
environment it now sits in.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Platform changes tone, cadence, and acceptable asks, not just length.

---

## What carries across, what doesn't

Not every failure mode transfers unchanged.

Length tolerance is not the same on LinkedIn as in email. A follow-up
cadence that's normal in a DM thread would read as aggressive sent as
email.

## Predict, then watch

Before the next slide: predict how a formally-worded cold email will read
if sent as a LinkedIn DM instead. Then check.

Sent as a DM, this reads as noticeably more formal than the channel
expects, and assumes an inbox the reader chose to check rather than a feed
they're scrolling past.

  <div class="email-block__header">
    <p><strong>From:</strong> [Sender]</p>
    <p><strong>To:</strong> [Recipient, sent as a LinkedIn DM]</p>
  </div>
  <div class="email-block__body">
    <p>Hey [Name], building something in [specific area], think it might be relevant to what you're doing at [Company]. Worth a quick look?</p>
  </div>
</div>

Same ask, adapted to the channel: shorter, no subject line, no "I hope this
finds you well," and phrased the way a message inside a feed actually gets
read rather than the way a message inside an inbox does.

---

## The point isn't three versions of everything

It's recognising which failure modes carry across platforms unchanged, and
which change shape entirely: tone, cadence, and acceptable asks, not just
length.

---

---

## Recap

- platform changes how quickly a message gets sized up, which is more
  than a delivery detail
- content that would pass on email can still fail purely on platform
  grounds
- adapting a message means checking tone, cadence, and ask, not just
  trimming it
