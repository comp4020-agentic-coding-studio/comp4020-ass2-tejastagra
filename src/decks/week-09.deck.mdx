# Same Words, Different Platform

Every failure named so far assumed email's particular constraints. What
happens when the same message moves somewhere else?

---

## Different environments, different triage

- a LinkedIn message is triaged inside a feed the reader didn't choose to
  open right now
- a DM arrives inside a channel with its own social expectations about
  response time
- an email arrives in an inbox the reader chose to check

Copying one into another and expecting it to read the same way ignores the
environment it now sits in.

---

## What carries across, what doesn't

Not every failure mode transfers unchanged.

Length tolerance is not the same on LinkedIn as in email. A follow-up
cadence that's normal in a DM thread would read as aggressive sent as
email.

Sent as a DM, this reads as noticeably more formal than the channel
expects, and assumes an inbox the reader chose to check rather than a feed
they're scrolling past.

---

## The point isn't three versions of everything

It's recognising which failure modes carry across platforms unchanged, and
which change shape entirely — tone, cadence, and acceptable asks, not just
length.

---

## Recap

- platform is a triage constraint, not a delivery detail
- content that would pass on email can still fail purely on platform
  grounds
- adapting a message means checking tone, cadence, and ask — not just
  trimming it
