# Why Cold Email Works (At Least Sometimes)

Most cold email gets deleted unread. This deck is about the exceptions.

---

## The claim

A cold email lives or dies on whether the reader can decide about it in a
few seconds.

The reader owes the sender nothing, has fifty of these, and a few seconds
each. Every failure named this semester is a specific way of losing that
few seconds.

---

## Case two: Janoch → investors

A first, short pitch for Mapistry went nowhere. A second attempt, sent two
years later, was longer and led with substance instead of charm.

Reported reply rate: 33% across the investors it went to.

Length wasn't the variable that changed. What changed was whether the
email gave the reader enough to decide with.

---

## Case three: Field's early Figma outreach

Not a funding ask. Dylan Field emailed former colleagues directly to find
Figma's first users — people who already had a thin, real reason to care.

The coldest emails that work often aren't fully cold.

---

## What the three have in common

- the reader wasn't persuaded — they were given enough to decide fast
- none of the three lean on charm to do the work
- each prices exactly what it's asking the reader to do

---

## Recap

- the reader decided fast, under time pressure, rather than evaluating the
  pitch on its merits
- "enough to decide with, fast" is the bar every week after this one tests
  a specific way of failing
- eleven distinct failures follow — one per week, none repeating another
