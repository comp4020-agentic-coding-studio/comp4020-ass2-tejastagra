# Process overview

## What I built

I built the cold email course I would have wanted as a student. SLOP3089 is formally "Writing to Strangers", though I call it "Nobody Owes You a Reply". Over twelve weeks it teaches students to write a cold email someone will answer. Its premise is that the reader skims a full inbox and decides in seconds whether to reply.

It has three assessment tasks, twelve slide decks and twelve Weekly Syncs (tutorials) with at least two exercises each, plus features I wish ANU courses had: a quick summary on every lecture page and navigation that gets you anywhere in a few clicks. I kept the given design template, because I value consistency across course websites. I teach it as convenor, with three tutors.

The harness is CLAUDE.md plus five topic files under `docs/`, with `spec/` checks enforcing the testable parts.

## How I got here

Before touching any code, I listed what the university courses I enjoyed had in common: content that was easy to follow, and assessments and slide decks I could find quickly. I shared both with the agent but kept one goal private: everything should be reachable within about five clicks. It was a user design decision, made by treating myself as the course's ideal persona, and it became my test of what Claude built.

Two of the best courses I have taken were graded pass or fail. I learned more in them because I was focused on understanding the material, and I was not chasing marks or mechanically following guidelines. I wanted this course to work the same way, and that choice is in the first version of the harness [`49c94f2`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/49c94f2). Claude had little to go on, and its drafts kept drifting toward a score for students to aim at, so I designed each assignment myself and Claude put them in place [`337379d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/337379d). I reframed the structure more than once, and every assessment page now says plainly that the percentages exist only so the platform can build [`bbeb73a`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/bbeb73a), [`f3b64ce`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/f3b64ce), [`73b1484`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/73b1484), [`adcd6b6`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/adcd6b6).

I planned the course in a separate chat on my own Claude account, which also wrote my structured prompts for Claude Code. I asked it to think like a YC founder with a business background, so the content was checked for practical usefulness before it reached Claude Code. I kept some of that persona on purpose in the first build: one tutor was an ex-founder whose startup had raised money on a nine-slide deck, a deliberate contrast to the site's dry voice [`203f4f0`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/203f4f0), [`7298b09`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/7298b09). The contrast stopped earning its place. One tutor shouting in a different register made a course about plain writing feel incoherent, so I rewrote him as a student with a real cold-email story and brought every bio into one voice [`5816820`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/5816820).

The first decks were generic and ugly, and the lecture pages repeated them in prose. A student needs something quick to scan and a reason to attend, so that became a rule: a short recap on the lecture page, with the full teaching and worked examples on the deck [`4afe0dc`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/4afe0dc). Every example got a proper email layout, which made the decks easier to follow and the course look purpose built [`2845731`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/2845731). I also added orientation slides at the start of week 1, the way real courses open [`79b622b`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/79b622b).

Partway through, the harness became the problem. Its rules contradicted each other, and the agent would only tell me it had broken one after doing the work. Trimming would have cut rules I still needed, so I split it into files for content, deck design, course structure and decision-making [`ae278ee`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/ae278ee), [`0302246`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/0302246), then removed duplicated rules [`942a735`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/942a735), [`d844914`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/d844914).

The split also let me run one agent per week, each reasoning against one small file. A single agent working through all twelve weeks built the first six carefully, then cut corners and argued back. Separate agents kept every week at the same standard [`44da9bd`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/44da9bd), [`da31bbd`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/da31bbd), [`64d6439`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/64d6439).

It still took a lot of back and forth. I knew what I wanted, but describing it with no gaps was hard, and Claude filled every gap with slop. I wrote each tutor's profile by hand in the end [`1a1e2df`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/1a1e2df), [`4c98e2f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/4c98e2f). Each fix Claude made for laptops broke mobile, and the next broke laptops. I told it to reduce the spacing between the logo and the first page link. That freed enough room, and it rendered correctly at both sizes [`6b7837f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-tejastagra/commit/6b7837f). Claude kept reaching for complicated fixes, and the simple one was obvious to me from looking at the page.
